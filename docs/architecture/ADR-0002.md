# ADR-0002: Speech Infrastructure — Separate Speech and Speaker Registries, Privacy by Architecture

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-07-12
**Deciders:** Founder (Bernard Abuto), AI CTO

## Context

Per the Engineering Roadmap's Track 1 (Language Programs), the stage
after Corpus is Speech. Per the Master Blueprint's Pillar III, the
Speech Data Platform is explicitly listed as a distinct platform from
the Language Corpus Platform, with different source types (broadcast
media, radio, field recordings, call centers) and different governance
needs — most critically, speech recordings involve real human speakers,
introducing consent and privacy concerns that do not exist for text
corpora.

Before writing any acquisition code, we needed to decide how speech
infrastructure should be architected to serve not just Dholuo and
Kiswahili, but hundreds of future languages over decades, per the
Roadmap's Engineering Doctrine: "We do not build features. We build
capabilities."

## Decision

We are building two new, separate frameworks rather than one combined
speech framework:

- **`speech-registry`** — governs recordings, sources, acquisition
  methods, provenance, technical metadata (duration, sample rate,
  format), and technical quality metrics.
- **`speaker-registry`** — governs pseudonymous speaker profiles and
  structured consent metadata.

Speakers and recordings are fundamentally different entities with
different lifecycles: a single speaker may contribute to multiple
datasets over many years, while a recording belongs to a specific
acquisition event. This mirrors the single-responsibility philosophy
already applied across every other framework (e.g. `dataset-framework`
vs. `corpus-registry`).

### Privacy as Architecture, Not Policy

The public engineering repository will never store personally
identifying information. This is enforced structurally, not through
contributor discipline:

- Speakers are referenced everywhere only by a pseudonymous
  `speaker_id`.
- The `speaker-registry` schema has no fields for name, phone number,
  email, national ID, photograph, precise location, or date of birth.
  These fields do not exist in the schema — they cannot be
  accidentally populated.
- Age is stored as a range (e.g. `18-24`), never an exact value.
- Location is stored at country/region/dialect-area granularity,
  never village-level or GPS coordinates.
- Any operational data required for real-world speaker recruitment
  (signed consent forms, payment records, contact details) belongs in
  a separate, secure operational system entirely outside this
  repository — never in Git history, ever.

### Consent as a Structured Governance Object

Consent is not a boolean. Every speaker's consent is recorded as a
structured object describing:

- Consent status (e.g. `obtained`, `implied_public_broadcast`,
  `not_applicable_synthetic`, `unknown_pending_review`)
- Scope of permitted use (e.g. research, model training,
  redistribution) — a speaker may consent to some uses and not others
- Consent agreement version
- Date consent was obtained

This mirrors the Copyright Discipline pattern already established in
`corpus-registry`'s ADR-0001 lineage: recordings tied to a speaker with
unresolved consent status must not be used to derive a dataset, exactly
as our Dholuo Bible corpus source remains blocked pending copyright
review.

## Consequences

**Positive:**
- Privacy protection does not depend on any individual contributor
  remembering a rule — the schema itself makes PII impossible to store.
- Speaker and recording lifecycles can evolve independently (e.g. a
  speaker's consent scope can be updated without touching any
  recording metadata).
- Consent policy can evolve over time (new consent agreement versions)
  without requiring architectural changes.
- Scales cleanly to community recording campaigns, institutional
  partnerships, and any future acquisition method without redesign.

**Negative:**
- Two frameworks instead of one adds cross-referencing complexity
  (a recording must reference a `speaker_id`, which must exist in a
  separate registry) — mitigated by the same validated cross-reference
  pattern already proven in M2.10's corpus-to-dataset chain test.
- Real-world operational identity management (the secure system
  holding actual PII) is explicitly out of scope for this repository
  and must be built separately before any real community recording
  campaign begins.

## Alternatives Considered

**Single combined `speech-framework`:** Rejected. Would conflate two
entities with different lifecycles and governance needs, contradicting
the single-responsibility pattern used everywhere else in the platform.

**Storing richer speaker profiles with restricted file visibility:**
Rejected. Relies on access-control policy rather than architectural
guarantee — a misconfiguration or future policy lapse could expose PII.
Per the Roadmap's long-term thinking principle, privacy must be
structurally impossible to violate, not merely restricted.
